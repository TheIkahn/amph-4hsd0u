## Week Equivalents

The Amphorean week is seven days like your own, but the rest day falls at the **end** of the week rather than the beginning. Plan your travel and your trade accordingly. A market that would be quiet on Mortdas back home is bustling on Eosdas here.

## The Four Divine Seasons

The Amphoreans do not divide their year into Winter, Spring, Summer, and Autumn the way you might. The Dawn Device governs the sky and softens the seasonal extremes you would know elsewhere. Instead, the year turns through four **divine seasons** named for groupings of the Titans.

- **Season of Fate** *(Gate, Balance, Evernight)*. The year begins. Quiet, contemplative, full of new contracts and new starts. The sun is dimmer in Evernight, the closest the Light Calendar comes to a winter.
- **Season of Pillar** *(Cultivation, Joy, Everday)*. Planting, abundance, the year's brightest light. Everday is the hottest stretch.
- **Season of Creation** *(Freedom, Reaping, Weaving)*. The harvest months. Kephale's rest gives way to Cerces' labor and Mnestia's festivals of memory.
- **Season of Calamity** *(Strife, Mourning, Fortune)*. The year closes. Storage, gladiatorial games, the burial of the dead, and the chaos of Zagreus' Spirit Day.

## The Five Hours of the Day

The Amphorean day is named in five Hours, set by the rhythm of the Dawn Device. Knowing the Hours will keep you from missing appointments, markets, and curfews.

- **Entry Hour** *(5:00 to 9:00)*. Dawn. Households wake, prayers are offered.
- **Lucid Hour** *(9:00 to 12:00)*. The mind is clearest. Scholarship, contracts, court.
- **Action Hour** *(12:00 to 17:00)*. The long productive block. Training, work, civic business.
- **Parting Hour** *(17:00 to 21:00)*. Evening farewells. Markets close. Social gatherings begin.
- **Curtain-Fall Hour** *(21:00 to 5:00)*. Night. Exclusive establishments, private business, and sleep.

If an Amphorean says "meet me at Lucid Hour," they mean any time within the block. If they say "the bell at Parting Hour," they mean its opening, which is to say five in the evening.

## A Note on the Year Itself

The Light Calendar's year count is its own. The number you see on a public proclamation, **4931**, is the count of years since the founding of civilization here, not since any event in Astrea. The Amphoreans do not use foreign era markers. If anyone asks you when you arrived, tell them by the month and the Hour, not the year.

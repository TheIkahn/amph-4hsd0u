Sir Caerwyn got MVP
Partly Cloudy, Humidity 64%, Percipitation 41%, Wind 9 m/s

The session starts with Yuichi and Franzen pulling aside and having a talk. Yuichi apologies for everything he has done. Franzen retorts that a simple apology will not work as the words he spoke had venom to them. Franzen warns that he stepped over the line and to not do it again. Franzen retorts as well that this is not a journey that anyone can make for him its something he has to figure out. 

Franzen walks back to the party while Yuichi sits and collects his thoughts. Franzen mentions that it will take some time to fully trust Yuichi again. Yuichi comes back and apologies to the group. Sir Caerwyn . Mayuri acknowledges his words but tells him that it takes more than words to undo the damage his done not just this time but other times before. Sir Caerwyn then musters everyone together and rally us back into the cave. before we go in, Caledor mentions to Yuichi that if his emotions get in the way, he will become a liability and Caledor would not want that. Then we all precede back into the cave. 

We look around and say we don’t want to go into the water and decide to hang around a stalactite. As we are walking around, a Behir jumps into the water while another behir is seen in the distance. Caledor has the bright idea to take out his rations this alerts the Behir to his Prescence however they start fighting each other over it. We strike them after they are done fighting. 

We quickly dispatch of the first Behir and move onto the second. which is also quickly dispatched. We walk around and find some Daer-Kron and some Crystal Quartz. We head to a river where the water looks slightly weird. Sir Caerwyn mentions about how there is suppose to be a soul tree. we eventually conclude that it must be the soul tree. 

```
_A single folded sheet of fine Janusopolian paper, very old, gone stiff and cloudy from years of damp. It was weighted to the stone shelf beneath a small, careful stack of soul coins, the way one weights an offering meant to stay. The ink has feathered but the hand is steady and elegant. It predates the city's fall._

---

To my Herodion.

The priests of Passage tell me I am doing this wrong. A letter is for the living, they say. The dead are not behind the door where you slip notes under. The river has carried you through already, and what I love is on the far side of a crossing I cannot make yet, and paper does not float that far.

I told them I am not writing to reach you. I am writing because the soultrees listen, and you always said this one was yours.

Do you remember saying that? You were younger than our son is now. You put your hand flat on the bark and declared it, the way you declared everything, as if the world had simply been waiting for you to assign it. This tree is mine. And the priest who was with us laughed and said child, the tree will outlive your grandchildren, and you said good, then it will remember me longest of anyone.

So I have come to your tree, and I am telling it things, so that it remembers.

I am telling it that you hated the dark and slept with the lamp lit your whole life and were never once ashamed of it. That you gave coin to every shrine on the Processional and could not have named half the Titans they belonged to. That you sang badly and often. That when the ferryman's bell rang for your mother you held my hand so hard it bruised, and that I wore the bruise like a ring for a week and was sorry when it faded.

The scholars say a soul is a seed, and that what we remember does not die with us but scatters, and lands, and comes up again in strangers. I used to think it a cold idea. Lately it comforts me. Somewhere downstream of you, someone has just been born who will sleep with the lamp lit and never know why. Someone will sing badly and often. They will not know your name. That is all right. I know your name. The tree knows your name.

When my own crossing comes, I have asked the priests for the rite at this bank, by this tree, so that whatever scatters of me lands close to whatever scattered of you. They tell me the river does not take requests. I have left coins to suggest that it might.

Wait for me where the water is quiet.

Your Iantha.

---

_Beneath the signature, in a different and much later hand, smaller, added by someone else entirely:_

Still here. Still quiet. I read it every shift I am posted to this bank. Whoever you both were, the tree is still standing. Thought you would want to know.
```
